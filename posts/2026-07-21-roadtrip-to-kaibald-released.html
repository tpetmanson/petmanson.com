It took me a long while to start writing this post, because so many things have been happening and the more I postpone, the more things will happen. So I better just start writing it right now and see how it goes. So for starters, let me just scream from the bottom of my lungs ...

FUCK YEAAAAAAH! IT IS DONE!!

"What is done?" you might ask if you don't use social media and live a peaceful life. Well, the fucking album is done! The one we've been working for the past year with Liisi and Don Tamman with our new hot-as-fuck supergroup Kaibald! It just got released exactly one month ago. So get the album while it is hot from bandcamp.

Anyways, I don't even know where to start as a lot of work and effort went into it :D. My last post regarding Kaibald was one week before we went to the studio. After that we have recorded the album, went to radio and TV and have given quite a few concerts already.

So there you have it, our entire lineup. Now that this is out of the way, let's talk a little about the music, because this is really where it gets interesting.

The entire theme of the album and in a way really what the band is about is just one thing, a concept, a principle that the music should sound like a movie soundtrack. Yes, a movie, and not a TV series btw :D.

Now this won't really tie it down too much, because there are many movie genres out there. You can have beautiful piano music from Ghibli movies up to heavy metal in some B-category action movie. But I've heard my friends describe Kaiblad as something that would fit a James Bond movie or perhaps something done by Tarantino. I admit I fucking love these comments as I really love Tarantino movies.

Regarding the music genre, I've heard people describe it as blues all the way, spaghetti western, desert blues, desert rock etc. By my own judgment, our music is 60% psychedelic rock, 40% blues and 100% of Kaibald sand mixed with our romantic souls graving for need to play music.

That said, psychedelic rock was really popular in the 1970s, which we definitely draw inspiration from and the bands we like draw inspiration from and which was the era which shaped the music we have today. Shut up! I know! This can be said right about every decade, but the goddamn 70s were special. Of course, it is also hard to deny the very strong blues influences flowing throughout the music. Hell, the opening track on the album is a blues song. But of course, many prefixes and suffixes could be attributed to Kaibald's music, depending on the track and the person making the assessment.

Okay, let's travel back in time to February 2026, when we went to Peeter Salmela's awesome spaceship in Tallinn.

Peeter Salmela aka "the Captain" behind his space ship console

It took us one and a half weeks to record all the stems for all the tracks on the album, except for some backing vocals that we recorded later at my place and sent in afterward. Each day, we got three to four songs in the can. Pretty impressive, right?

So what was the process you ask? Well, I'm gonna tell you right away

Day 1 - we arrived at the studio, I think Liisi and Don were there before everybody else and did some set up on the drums already. The drums were set up in the first room next to the entrance. One thing Peeter Salmela's studio is known for is the superior drum sound recordings. I don't know if this is an official statement, but word on the street is that there is no better place for recording drums in Estonia.

Liisi did get a special spot next to the spaceship console right next to the captain. And as a bonus she could peek Don through the window.

Kristjan got a dedicated room for him and his Precision bass.

While others were setting up the gear, me and Pets were probably smoking outside and waiting our turn. In fact, we two are the most unhealthy musicians in the band, followed closely by Liisi-Mai.

I used a Twin Reverb amp throughout the entire album, which you can see below in the image.

Peeter had a Marshall amp, I don't remember which model. Kristjan used a Trinity Trip Top amp. I've never heard of it before, but it sounded great!

I only brought three guitars, Kristjan brought one, but Pets had at least four guitars. Also the captain had one in the studio. I think we used a total of nine different guitars on the album, but could have been more. I really hope I have them all photographed here.

The full assortment of guitars used on the album.

So when we got gear set up, we started recording pretty much straight away. I don't remember what the first song was, but I think we got it in the can + another half of a song maybe.

After we had recorded several takes, we usually gathered in the control room to listen to what we had done. If we were satisfied we the material, we usually spent a little bit of time to make some edits or choose specific takes for parts of the song.

Days 2 to 5 we just recorded about three to four songs a day using the same process.

Days 6 - 8 is a all a haze right now. One day was dedicated fully to recording the hammond and rhodes. Another day for the trumpet, violin and some back vocals. And perhaps one day was dedicated to anything missing.

So yeah, pretty crazy stuff! It took a few months to actually send in all the missing back vocals and finish the mixing. But we made it!

Before the album presentation concert, Liisi and Don organized us some media coverage, we gave interviews on the radio and also performed on the TV.

Kaibald touring Estonian Radio shows.
